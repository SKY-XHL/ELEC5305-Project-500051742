01 · Research question

How much labelled data does a useful audio representation need?

How does ESC-50 classification performance scale with the amount of task-specific labelled data when using MFCC, Log-Mel, or frozen Microsoft CLAP representations under a common linear-probe protocol?

DatasetESC-50balanced environmental sound benchmark
Label budgets1 → 32clips per class, plus zero-shot CLAP
MetricsAccuracy + F1with class-level error analysis
Scope

The contribution is a reproducible analysis of task-specific label efficiency—not a new foundation model and not a claim of broad out-of-domain generalisation.

Strong transfer before the label budget becomes large.

A

Five labels per class recover 98.87% of full-data CLAP accuracy.

Moving from zero-shot to five-shot adds 2.60 percentage points in accuracy while using 84.4% fewer task-specific labels than the full training condition.

  • Zero-shot93.85%
  • Five-shot96.45%
  • Full97.55%
B

Local supervision corrects semantically plausible zero-shot errors.

Five labelled clips per class sharply reduce several dominant confusion pairs. For example, hen → rooster falls from 42.5% to 0.5%, while crickets → insects falls from 30.0% to 0%.

Interpretation

Text-aligned semantics provide a strong starting point; a small amount of local acoustic evidence resolves dataset-specific class boundaries.

05 · Interpretation

The result is strong, but its boundary matters.

What worked

Frozen semantic representations transfer efficiently.

CLAP separates most ESC-50 categories before task-specific supervision and benefits further from a small labelled set.

Engineering cost

The accuracy gain is not computationally free.

The 1,024-D embedding is larger than either pooled classical feature; CPU extraction took 1,016 seconds for 2,000 clips.

Evidence boundary

ESC-50 performance is not universal generalisation.

Zero-shot means no task-specific labels were used for fitting. It does not prove that pretraining contained no related audio.

Project conclusion

Representation choice dominates the low-label regime on ESC-50.

Frozen CLAP embeddings substantially reduce task-specific labelling requirements under this controlled protocol. Five labels per class approach full-data CLAP performance, while error analysis shows how local supervision refines the zero-shot semantic boundary.
